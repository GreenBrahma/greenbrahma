What is the idea

A live scorecard of how often AI answers name a business, embedded on a page, under somebody else’s brand.

The problem it names

When someone asks ChatGPT for the best personal injury lawyer in Kansas City, or the best commercial roofer, the model goes and reads the open web and writes an answer naming three or four businesses. Everyone else is invisible, and has no idea.

There is no rank to check. No console, no position report, nothing in Google Analytics. A business can be doing well in search and simply not exist in the answer a buyer actually reads.

What we built

An engine that asks the models the questions a real buyer would ask, with their web search switched on, and scores what comes back. It reports four things that matter:

  • Unprompted mention rate. How often the business is named when the question never mentions it. Asking “is Acme good?” and getting “Acme” back is an echo, not visibility, and most tools count it anyway.
  • Average position in the list the model returns, and share of voice against named competitors.
  • The sources the models read before answering, ranked by how much they influenced the answer.
  • Spread across repeat runs, because these models are not deterministic and a single run is not a measurement.

The delivery: a widget, not a report

Two lines of code on a page. It renders a live scorecard under whoever is reselling it. No login for the end client, no monthly export, no GreenBrahma anywhere on it.

Everything else — the prompts, the model runs, the citation parsing, the scoring — happens on our side and refreshes on its own.

That last panel is the product

It is not a score. It is a work order. If the models are building their answer out of four directories and barely touching the client’s own site, you now know exactly which four properties to go fix, in priority order — and that fixing is billable work on top of the subscription.

Where they are beatable

Not on features. They are further along than we are: seven engines, fourteen report sections, Word export, scheduled delivery, team roles. They also offer clients a read-only live dashboard, so “they only send PDFs” would be wrong.

The opening is where the number lives. Theirs is a destination the client has to be invited into and log in to. Ours is two lines on a page the client already owns.

A login is a destination. An embed is furniture. Most client-facing reporting dies because somebody has to go and look at it, and eventually nobody does. That is the part worth attacking — not their feature list, which we will not out-build this year.

The part that is genuinely hard, and done

Anyone can call four APIs. The hard part is scoring the answers honestly, and the off-the-shelf tools do not.

We tested a firm whose domain shares a word with an unrelated Spanish term. A commercial tool reported 93% mention rate and 86% share of voice. The real answer was that no model had ever heard of them — it was matching the wrong word entirely.

Our engine caught it. It separates unprompted mentions from echoes, detects “I don’t have reliable information about them” so a disclaimer never scores as a win, and runs every prompt several times to report the spread. A number that survives a client meeting is the product.

How do we activate the idea

Prove it on one real account, package what worked, then sell the package. In that order.

Where we actually are

Straight about this: the engine runs and the scoring is solid, and it is being used in anger on a live account right now. The widget is being packaged. There is no logo wall and there are no founding partners yet — there are open slots and a working product.

That is either the wrong moment to look at this or exactly the right one, and which it is depends on whether we want to be early or safe.

Under five minutes per client once the account exists. That is what makes this a product line rather than another service we would have to staff.
