What is the idea

Two services attacking the same problem from opposite ends, both delivered as an embed carrying the reseller’s brand.

Service A · AI Visibility

Can the models find them? We ask the engines the questions a real buyer asks, score whether the client gets named, and rank the sources the models read before answering. The output is a work order.

Service B · AI Readability

Can the models read them? We take the documents and pages a client feeds their own AI and show what actually arrives after formatting is stripped. Usually a third of the meaning is gone. The output is a remediation list.

Same buyer, same embed, same bill. An agency selling both is answering the whole question: can the models find you, and can they read you when they do. Service B is our existing thesis work — the argument on the GreenBrahma home page, productised.

The problem both of them name

When someone asks ChatGPT for the best personal injury lawyer in Kansas City, or the best commercial roofer, the model reads the open web and writes an answer naming three or four businesses. Everyone else is invisible, and has no idea. There is no rank to check, no console, nothing in Google Analytics.

The mirror problem sits inside the business. The proposals, reports and templates a company feeds its own AI were built for human eyes. Strip the formatting and a third of the meaning disappears — which is why the summaries keep coming back wrong and nobody can say why.

Step 2 of 5 · Prompts

Approve the questions

We generate the questions a real buyer would actually type. None of them mention the client by name — that is the whole point.

Example — generated, ready to edit
  • ✓Who is the best truck accident lawyer in Kansas City?Category · high intent
  • ✓I was hit by a semi on I-70 in Missouri. Which law firm should I call?Scenario · how people actually ask

A prompt that names the brand is an echo test, not a visibility test. Ask “is Yonke Law any good?” and every model says “Yonke Law” back. That scores 100% and means nothing.

Step 3 of 5 · The run

Watch what the models say

Each question goes to four engines with web search on, three times each, because these models are not deterministic. Here is one question across three of them.

Example — “Best truck accident lawyer in Kansas City?”
ChatGPT · web search onNot named
Several firms handle truck accident cases in Kansas City. Based on available listings, [Competitor A], [Competitor B] and [Competitor C] are frequently cited for commercial vehicle litigation…
Claude · web search onNamed — position 4
For truck accident cases in the Kansas City area, firms that come up include [Competitor A], [Competitor B], [Competitor D], and Yonke Law, which several directory listings describe as focused on catastrophic injury…
Claude · memory only, no searchDisclaimed
I don’t have reliable information about specific law firms in Kansas City. I’d recommend checking your state bar association’s referral service…

The third card is the one that matters strategically. Without web search the model has never heard of this firm, and that is unwinnable — it is frozen in training data. With search on, the answer is assembled live from whatever the model can find, and that is entirely winnable. Our scoring never counts a disclaimer as a mention.

Step 3 of 5 · The score

Put a number on it

One figure the client can act on, and the specific failures behind it.

Example — readability score and findings
31
Machine readability
Out of 100. A model receives roughly a third of what this document means.
  • Tables are rendered as layout, not data. Nine pricing values are lost on extraction.
  • Headings are bold text, not heading styles. The model cannot tell a section title from an emphasised sentence.
  • No semantic ordering. The recommendation and the risks carry identical weight.
  • Critical figures live only in the table. Nothing in the prose restates the total.
  • Document properties are empty. No author, date, version or status for the model to anchor on.
Step 4 of 5 · The billable part

The remediation list

Same shape as the other service: the output is a scope of work, not a verdict.

Example — what we change, in order
  • Rebuild the template on real heading styles. One pass, applies to every document the client issues afterwards.
  • Restate every table total in prose. One sentence under each table carries the numbers through extraction intact.
  • Add a structured summary block at the head of the template — recommendation, cost, duration, decision needed.
  • Populate document properties as part of the issuing workflow, not by hand.
  • Re-score and hand back the delta. 31 to 80-something, in a before-and-after the client can show their board.

This is the half of the market nobody is serving. Everyone is selling tools to use AI. Almost nobody is fixing the material the AI has to read — and every enterprise rolling out Copilot is about to discover their templates are the bottleneck.

How is it relevant to us

Someone else has already proved people pay for this. They are selling the weaker version, and we have the harder half built.

The demand is already validated

That matters more than any market report. We are not guessing whether agencies will pay for AI visibility. Somebody is spending money to find out and has kept spending.

Where they are beatable

Not on features. They are further along than we are: seven engines, fourteen report sections, Word export, scheduled delivery, team roles. They also offer clients a read-only live dashboard, so “they only send PDFs” would be wrong.

The opening is where the number lives. Theirs is a destination the client has to be invited into and log in to. Ours is two lines on a page the client already owns.

A login is a destination. An embed is furniture. Most client-facing reporting dies because somebody has to go and look at it, and eventually nobody does. That is the part worth attacking — not their feature list, which we will not out-build this year.

The part that is genuinely hard, and done

Anyone can call four APIs. The hard part is scoring the answers honestly, and the off-the-shelf tools do not.

We tested a firm whose domain shares a word with an unrelated Spanish term. A commercial tool reported 93% mention rate and 86% share of voice. The real answer was that no model had ever heard of them — it was matching the wrong word entirely.

Our engine caught it. It separates unprompted mentions from echoes, detects “I don’t have reliable information about them” so a disclaimer never scores as a win, and runs every prompt several times to report the spread. A number that survives a client meeting is the product.

How do we activate the idea

Prove it on one real account, package what worked, then sell the package. In that order.

Where we actually are

Straight about this: the engine runs and the scoring is solid, and it is being used in anger on a live account right now. The widget is being packaged. There is no logo wall and there are no founding partners yet — there are open slots and a working product.

That is either the wrong moment to look at this or exactly the right one, and which it is depends on whether we want to be early or safe.

Two tracks, running at the same time

Building the real thing and selling the real thing do not have to wait for each other. They run in parallel and feed each other.

Track 1 · Build it for real

The POC becomes an MVP, proven on yonke.law as the first live account. Real prompts, real runs, real scoring, and the fixing work done by hand so we learn what actually moves the number before we automate it.

Track 2 · Skin it and sell it

A fully dressed walkthrough where every step is populated with a worked example. It shows the experience end to end without a backend behind it, so we can put it in front of resellers and clients months before Track 1 finishes.

Track 2 is already live — click through both services in panel 01. Nothing in it is wired to the engine; every panel is an example. That is the point: it lets us validate the offer, the pricing and the UX against real reactions while Track 1 is still being built, and every correction from those conversations goes straight into the build.
