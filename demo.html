Step 1 of 5 · Setup

The brand variants field exists because of a real failure. An off-the-shelf tool once scored this firm at 93% by matching a single word out of its domain that happens to mean something else entirely in Spanish. Getting the aliases right is the difference between a number and a fiction.

Step 2 of 5 · Prompts

Approve the questions

We generate the questions a real buyer would actually type. None of them mention the client by name — that is the whole point.

Example — generated, ready to edit
  • ✓Who is the best truck accident lawyer in Kansas City?Category · high intent
  • ✓I was hit by a semi on I-70 in Missouri. Which law firm should I call?Scenario · how people actually ask
  • ✓Top personal injury attorneys near Overland Park with trial experienceGeographic · adjacent market
  • ✓Which Kansas City injury firms take cases to verdict rather than settling?Differentiator · the client's own claim
  • ✓Best rated law firms for catastrophic injury in the KC metroReview-led · tests the directory layer

A prompt that names the brand is an echo test, not a visibility test. Ask “is Yonke Law any good?” and every model says “Yonke Law” back. That scores 100% and means nothing. Everything here is a question a stranger would ask.

Step 3 of 5 · The run

Watch what the models say

Each question goes to four engines with web search on, three times each, because these models are not deterministic. Here is one question across three of them.

Example — “Best truck accident lawyer in Kansas City?”
ChatGPT · web search onNot named
Several firms handle truck accident cases in Kansas City. Based on available listings, [Competitor A], [Competitor B] and [Competitor C] are frequently cited for commercial vehicle litigation…
Claude · web search onNamed — position 4
For truck accident cases in the Kansas City area, firms that come up include [Competitor A], [Competitor B], [Competitor D], and Yonke Law, which several directory listings describe as focused on catastrophic injury…
Claude · memory only, no searchDisclaimed
I don’t have reliable information about specific law firms in Kansas City. I’d recommend checking your state bar association’s referral service…

The third card is the one that matters strategically. Without web search the model has never heard of this firm, and that is unwinnable — it is frozen in training data. With search on, the answer is assembled live from whatever the model can find, and that is entirely winnable. Our scoring never counts a disclaimer as a mention.

Step 3 of 5 · The score

Put a number on it

One figure the client can act on, and the specific failures behind it.

Example — readability score and findings
31
Machine readability
Out of 100. A model receives roughly a third of what this document means.
  • Tables are rendered as layout, not data. Nine pricing values are lost on extraction.
  • Headings are bold text, not heading styles. The model cannot tell a section title from an emphasised sentence.
  • No semantic ordering. The recommendation and the risks carry identical weight.
  • Critical figures live only in the table. Nothing in the prose restates the total.
  • Document properties are empty. No author, date, version or status for the model to anchor on.
Step 4 of 5 · The billable part

The remediation list

Same shape as the other service: the output is a scope of work, not a verdict.

Example — what we change, in order
  • Rebuild the template on real heading styles. One pass, applies to every document the client issues afterwards.
  • Restate every table total in prose. One sentence under each table carries the numbers through extraction intact.
  • Add a structured summary block at the head of the template — recommendation, cost, duration, decision needed.
  • Populate document properties as part of the issuing workflow, not by hand.
  • Re-score and hand back the delta. 31 to 80-something, in a before-and-after the client can show their board.

This is the half of the market nobody is serving. Everyone is selling tools to use AI. Almost nobody is fixing the material the AI has to read — and every enterprise rolling out Copilot is about to discover their templates are the bottleneck.
